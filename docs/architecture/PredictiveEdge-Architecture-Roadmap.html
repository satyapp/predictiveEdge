1. Purpose and operating model

PredictiveEdge begins with realtime decision support. A trader expresses intent; the CORE AI engine evaluates point-in-time market context, constructs a risk-controlled trade plan, and continuously reassesses the accepted trade. Paper trading and historical replay will reuse the same decision contracts and calculation logic when reliable historical data is available.

1

Intent first

Analysis is triggered by explicit trader intent, not by autonomous order placement.

2

Point-in-time truth

Every decision records the market snapshot, feature versions, model version, and risk state used.

3

Guarded execution

Only the Execution Gateway can submit an order, after authorization and risk approval.

4

Shared intelligence

Realtime, paper, and backtest modes share indicator, strategy, risk, and outcome contracts.

Execution principle: the AI recommends and explains. Risk controls authorize. The trader or an explicitly approved policy triggers execution.

2. Kafka-enabled platform architecture

The command path remains short and controlled, while Kafka distributes durable market, decision, order, position, risk, session, and guardian events to independent consumers.

3. Business flow after trader intent

The business flow separates recommendation, approval, execution, monitoring, and learning. Every rejection or no-trade outcome is a valid, explainable decision.

4. Component model

Components communicate through explicit application ports and versioned event contracts. Infrastructure adapters implement broker, database, Kafka, cache, and historical-store access without leaking those technologies into domain logic.

5. Realtime data and indicator requirements

The broker provides trades, quotes, instrument metadata, and order services. PredictiveEdge owns candle construction and indicator calculation to guarantee consistent, testable values across realtime and replay modes.

Required live streams

Trades or tick data, best bid/ask or market depth where available, instrument metadata, trading calendar, corporate actions, and order/fill updates.

Calculated internally

Forming and closed 1m/5m/15m candles, VWAP, EMA, RSI, ATR, pivots, Supertrend, volume features, volatility, trend, momentum, and data-quality flags.

Context enrichment

Index and sector direction, relative strength, market breadth, scheduled events, news, session phase, gap state, liquidity, and portfolio exposure.

7. Non-negotiable architecture rules

One execution path

Paper and live orders must pass through the Execution Gateway after authorization and risk approval.

Guardian recommends

Trade Guardian publishes reassessment recommendations; it cannot place, amend, or cancel orders directly.

Database is authoritative

PostgreSQL owns domain state and audit records. Redis is rebuildable and never the sole system of record.

Outbox and inbox

Publish only committed events. Deduplicate consumer work before committing the Kafka offset.

Version everything

Event schemas, indicator definitions, feature sets, strategies, models, risk policies, and decision explanations are versioned.

No look-ahead

Realtime and replay decisions may use only data available at the recorded decision timestamp.

Freshness is evidence

Every input carries event time, ingest time, source, quality status, and freshness. Stale critical evidence forces abstention.

Forming bars are provisional

Strategies must explicitly state whether they accept forming-candle evidence or require closed bars.

Learning is governed

Outcome data can propose improvements, but model and strategy promotion requires validation and approval.

8. Delivery roadmap

The sequence prioritizes safe realtime decision support, then supervised execution, and only then historical replay and training.

Foundation

Phase 1 — Contracts and reliable market context

Canonical instruments, trading calendar, market-data gateway, observation quality, candle generation, indicator parity tests, PostgreSQL outbox/inbox, Kafka topic contracts, and observability.

Realtime AI

Phase 2 — Trader-intent decision support

Intent authorization, decision orchestration, market/chart/strategy intelligence, evidence snapshots, trade/no-trade explanations, risk assessment, and portal presentation.

Controlled execution

Phase 3 — Broker execution and portfolio truth

Execution Gateway, live broker adapter, idempotent order lifecycle, reconciliation, positions, exposure, P&L, kill switches, and operational alerts.

Continuous supervision

Phase 4 — Trade Guardian

Position thesis, event-driven health checks, protective recommendations, escalation policy, trader notifications, audit timeline, and incident recovery.

Future capability

Phase 5 — Paper trading and historical replay

Licensed historical data, corporate-action adjustments, deterministic event clock, simulated exchange, slippage/latency/fees, replayable Kafka-compatible events, and data-quality certification.

Governed learning

Phase 6 — Backtesting and CORE AI training

Trader-intent-triggered replay, walk-forward evaluation, leakage controls, outcome attribution, reproducible datasets, champion/challenger validation, and governed model promotion.

9. Definition of architectural success

Reproducible

Given the same intent, event-time inputs, feature versions, and policies, the system can reconstruct why a decision was made.

Safe to abstain

Missing, conflicting, stale, or low-quality evidence produces a clear no-trade result instead of false certainty.

Mode-consistent

The same domain rules and calculations operate in realtime, paper, and backtest modes; only the clock, data source, and execution adapter change.
